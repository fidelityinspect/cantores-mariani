How to sing the propers

What the propers are

Every Mass has its own three chants, assigned by the Church: the Introit as the procession enters, the Offertory while the gifts are prepared, and the Communion during Holy Communion. Their words come from Scripture, mostly the Psalms, and they change every Sunday. These are the chants the Council gave "pride of place." The Simple English Propers set them in English, in plain chant that a beginner can learn in a week.

Reading the page
  • The music has four lines, not five. The clef at the start marks one line as C (or F); every other note follows from it.
  • Notes are square. Two notes stacked on one syllable: sing the lower one first.
  • There is no beat to count. Chant moves at the speed of careful speech, so the words set the rhythm.
  • The * in the antiphon marks where everyone joins: the cantor sings up to it alone, then all sing the rest.
How each chant goes at Mass
  1. The cantor begins the antiphon; all join at the *.
  2. The cantor (or half the schola) sings a psalm verse on a simple repeated tone. The * in a verse is a short breath at the middle.
  3. All sing the antiphon again. Repeat verses and antiphon until the procession or Communion is finished, ending with "Glory be" and a last antiphon.
Your week, step by step
  1. Read the words aloud slowly, as if proclaiming them. That is the rhythm of the chant.
  2. Watch the practice video three times: listen, then hum along, then sing along with the score open.
  3. Sing it alone once without the video. Mark any spot that trips you and go back to just that line.
  4. Come to rehearsal ready to sing it through. We put it together there and sing it on Sunday.
